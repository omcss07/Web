Spike Jonze

Hers, His... What do these words define?, is it the feeling of belonging, or are they just terms to show that you belong to someone or something, or vice versa? How does he feel about her? Why does everything keep falling apart? Does growing up together, maturing together, and changing together have to mean falling apart? There's that feeling of helplessness, like a bird watching its children fly away without even saying thanks. It's infuriating. But what can they do about it, should they accept that others change and fell apart, or should they fight for love? If everyone changes, how can them ever come to like someone? What's the true nature behind all? If humans do love each other, why couldn't they make a sacrifice to save that little flame they have inside, lit by all the emotions and moments they share together? It's hard, horrendous, and mostly crude, that feeling that changes everything, feeling naked in a room full of people you love laughing at you. Love is real, but it takes both lovers an equal sacrifice and equal compromise, to love someone more than to respect that someone is to feel as equal, not superior, not inferior, just, equal. But sometimes "If I've got to, I'm going to," no matter what I lose, if life or something important depends on others' wellbeing and can be sacrificed without direct physical deterioration or psychological worsening of mind. Falling apart feels like being on an extremely rough river, being dragged by the currents, with that extreme feeling of hope, that unusual amount of love, feels like swimming a whole sea alone, being aware of the terrifying truth that's lying beneath one. Is it worth it, to find that someone, to fight for that someone that is "loved"? Is there a point to love them even if they don't? How could anyone notice who really loves and who doesn't if human beings always want to benefit from others? It's coarse, imagine sliding down through a spiky slide, thinking about what's at the end, "the happiest of all realities"; is that feeling real? But at the end of the day you still sliding and will never know what’s behind it all. "A relationship is a dance where two souls intertwine, navigating the rhythm of vulnerability, trust, and growth, finding harmony in the melody of shared experiences." The only melody heard is the annoying tinnitus of someone sucking out your soul for their own benefit. Disgusting, sickening, sick. Why does human nature lead to selfishness? And if it does, why couldn't we work out a new way of thinking or a new society where everyone fits in and everyone deserves to be helped and, more importantly, has in some way the right to help? Is it our brain's way of functioning? It seems like a different person living inside someone. Does unconditional love really exist? Is someone supposed to be a sucker for validation? What's really the point here? To exist? To accept everything as it is? Or to grow and evolve as one? To not judge, to make others feel happy and, most importantly, to end up with that feeling of satisfaction often confused with immediate pleasure. That satisfaction that fulfills and exceeds the needs. Every relationship has stages, the idea of romance, the bridge and the two paths, you can stay, or, you can leave. Once they reach the bridge, there’s a breach. The idea of knowing someone, what else should they do? That’s the real challenge, knowing that someone knows them it’s not enough, they have the urge to keep knowing, but what it’s knowing? If we know everything what’s the point of life? That fragile bridge it’s the prove they both love each other, no matter what happen they know what’s up for both.
